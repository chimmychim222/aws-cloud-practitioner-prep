If you've started exploring AWS certifications, you've almost certainly hit this question early: should you go for the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) first, or jump straight to the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate? It's one of the most common dilemmas for people entering the cloud space, and the answer isn't one-size-fits-all.

This guide breaks down both certifications honestly — what they test, who they're designed for, and how to decide which path makes sense for your situation right now.

What Each Certification Actually Is

AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)

The Cloud Practitioner is AWS's Foundational-level certification. It's designed to validate broad, conceptual knowledge of the AWS Cloud — not hands-on technical depth. You don't need to know how to build infrastructure to pass it; you need to understand what AWS is, why organizations use it, and how core services, pricing, and security concepts fit together.

The exam consists of 65 questions (50 scored, 15 unscored — you can't tell which is which), lasts 90 minutes, and requires a passing score of 700 out of 1000 on a scaled scoring system. The registration fee is $100 USD at the time of writing, though AWS fees are subject to change.

The four exam domains are: - Cloud Concepts (24%) — the value proposition of cloud, the Well-Architected Framework, the Cloud Adoption Framework, migration strategies - Security and Compliance (30%) — shared responsibility model, IAM, threat detection tools, compliance concepts - Cloud Technology and Services (34%) — compute, storage, databases, networking, and core AWS services at a conceptual level - Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%) — pricing models, cost management tools, support plan tiers

If you want a full breakdown of what's on the exam, the official exam guide is the best place to start.

AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate (SAA-C03)

The Solutions Architect – Associate sits one level higher, at Associate level. It tests your ability to design secure, resilient, high-performing, and cost-optimized architectures on AWS. This is a hands-on, scenario-driven exam — you'll be presented with real-world business problems and asked which architecture or combination of services best solves them.

It covers topics like VPC design, load balancing, auto scaling, storage selection trade-offs, database replication strategies, IAM policies, and cost-optimization patterns — all at a level of depth the Cloud Practitioner doesn't require.

The exam is longer, goes deeper, and generally requires meaningful hands-on experience with AWS services to pass comfortably.

The Core Difference: Breadth vs. Depth

Think of it this way:

  • Cloud Practitioner = wide and shallow. You touch everything but don't go deep on any of it.
  • Solutions Architect – Associate = narrower scope, but significantly deeper. You're expected to know why you'd choose one architecture over another, not just that the services exist.

A Cloud Practitioner candidate needs to know that Amazon RDS supports relational databases and DynamoDB is a fully managed NoSQL service. A Solutions Architect candidate needs to know when to use each, how to replicate them across regions for resilience, and what the performance and cost implications of each choice are.

Who Should Start With Cloud Practitioner?

You're New to Cloud Entirely

If cloud computing is genuinely new territory — you've never worked with AWS, Azure, or GCP in any meaningful way — starting with the Cloud Practitioner is almost always the right call. It gives you the vocabulary and mental framework that makes everything else easier to learn. Skipping it and diving into Solutions Architect often leads to frustration, because you're trying to learn how to build things before you understand what you're building with or why.

You're in a Non-Technical Role

The Cloud Practitioner was specifically designed to be accessible to people in business, finance, project management, sales, and other non-engineering roles. If your job involves working alongside cloud teams — reading AWS invoices, participating in migration planning meetings, or evaluating vendor proposals — the Cloud Practitioner gives you exactly the level of fluency you need without requiring you to configure a VPC.

You Want to Validate Your Foundation Before Going Deeper

Even experienced developers sometimes find value in starting with Cloud Practitioner. It forces you to look at AWS from a business and architectural perspective rather than just "the services I use day-to-day," and it's a low-stakes way to get comfortable with AWS exam format before sitting a harder test.

Who Should Consider Skipping Straight to Solutions Architect?

You Already Have Hands-On AWS Experience

If you've been building on AWS for a year or more — deploying EC2 instances, configuring S3 buckets, setting up IAM roles, working with RDS or Lambda — you may already know most of what the Cloud Practitioner tests. In that case, going straight to Solutions Architect is a reasonable move. The foundational exam won't teach you much you don't already know.

You're an Experienced Infrastructure or Systems Engineer Moving to Cloud

Network engineers, sysadmins, and on-premises infrastructure specialists often find the Cloud Practitioner too introductory. If you already understand concepts like DNS, load balancing, firewalls, and redundancy from an on-premises context, you can often map that knowledge to AWS fairly quickly and go straight for the Associate level.

Your Target Role Specifically Requires It

Some hiring managers treat the Cloud Practitioner as entry-level validation and the Solutions Architect – Associate as the bar for technical cloud roles. If the job you're targeting explicitly lists Solutions Architect as preferred or required, spending time on Cloud Practitioner first may delay your goal rather than advance it.

The Case for Doing Both (in Order)

Here's something the "skip it" camp sometimes overlooks: the Cloud Practitioner isn't just a stepping stone — it actively makes you better at the Solutions Architect exam.

The domain weightings of the Cloud Practitioner mean you spend real time on security and compliance (30%), billing and pricing (12%), and cloud concepts (24%). These aren't throwaway topics. Solutions Architect candidates who skipped foundational study often struggle with cost-optimization questions and shared responsibility edge cases precisely because they never formally studied them.

Doing Cloud Practitioner first also means you have a certification credential to show while you're still working toward the more valuable Associate-level badge — which can matter if you're actively job searching or trying to make an internal case for a role change.

Career Relevance: Setting Realistic Expectations

The Cloud Practitioner alone is unlikely to land you a cloud engineering role. It signals awareness, not technical capability. Where it genuinely helps:

  • Career changers using it as proof of commitment to learning cloud
  • Non-technical professionals who need credibility in cloud conversations
  • Students and recent graduates building a credentials foundation
  • Existing IT staff cross-training into cloud responsibilities

The Solutions Architect – Associate carries more weight for technical roles — cloud architects, DevOps engineers, infrastructure engineers, and similar positions. Employers hiring for these roles generally treat the Associate badge as meaningful evidence of practical AWS knowledge.

Neither certification guarantees a specific salary or job offer. Certifications are one signal among many — experience, portfolio, and soft skills matter too. Treat certifications as credibility builders, not magic keys.

A Practical Decision Framework

Ask yourself these three questions:

  1. How much hands-on AWS experience do I have?
  1. What role am I targeting?
  1. How much time do I have to prepare?

Once you know which path you're on, practice questions are one of the most effective ways to measure and close your knowledge gaps before exam day.

FAQ

Is the AWS Cloud Practitioner worth it if I already have IT experience?

It depends on your goals. If you're targeting a technical cloud role, the Solutions Architect – Associate will carry more weight with employers. However, if you're new to AWS specifically — even with general IT experience — the Cloud Practitioner is a useful way to build cloud-specific vocabulary and pass your first AWS exam before tackling a harder one.

Can I take the Solutions Architect exam without doing Cloud Practitioner first?

Yes. AWS does not require you to hold Cloud Practitioner before sitting the Solutions Architect – Associate. The Cloud Practitioner is not a formal prerequisite. Whether you should skip it depends on your current knowledge level and experience.

How hard is the Cloud Practitioner exam compared to Solutions Architect?

The Cloud Practitioner is significantly easier than Solutions Architect. Cloud Practitioner tests conceptual understanding; Solutions Architect tests applied, scenario-based architectural judgment. Most candidates with solid study habits find Cloud Practitioner manageable with a few weeks of focused preparation.

What's the passing score for the Cloud Practitioner exam?

The passing score is 700 out of 1000 on a scaled scoring system (range: 100–1000). This is not a raw percentage — it's a scaled score, meaning the number of correct answers required can vary slightly depending on question difficulty.

How long does it take to prepare for each exam?

Preparation time varies widely by individual background. Cloud Practitioner candidates often report needing anywhere from two weeks to two months of study. Solutions Architect typically requires more time, especially if you're building hands-on lab experience alongside your reading. There's no universal answer — your starting point matters more than anything else.

Do certifications expire?

AWS certifications are valid for three years from the date you pass. After that, you'll need to recertify to keep the credential active. This applies to both Cloud Practitioner and Solutions Architect.
