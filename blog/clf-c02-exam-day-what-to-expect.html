Walking into exam day without knowing what to expect is one of the most avoidable sources of test anxiety. The AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) is a well-structured, predictable exam — and that predictability is your advantage. This guide breaks down exactly what the exam looks like, how time works in your favour, and what to do in the days and hours before you sit down.

The Exam at a Glance

Before diving into strategy, lock in the basics so nothing surprises you at the testing centre or on your online proctored screen.

Scored vs. Unscored Questions

Of the 65 questions you see, 15 are unscored "pilot" questions that AWS uses to evaluate them for future exams. You cannot tell which questions are scored and which are not, so treat every single question as if it counts. There is no penalty for guessing — always answer every question.

Question Formats

  • Multiple-choice: One correct answer from four options. Eliminate obvious distractors first, then choose the best remaining answer.
  • Multiple-response: Exactly two correct answers from five options. The question will tell you to select two. There is no partial credit, so you must get both right.

When you see a multiple-response question, resist the temptation to select a third answer "just in case." It won't help and it locks in your choices.

Domain Weightings: Where the Points Live

The exam draws questions from four domains. Knowing where the weight sits helps you spend study time proportionally.

DomainTopicWeight
1Cloud Concepts24%
2Security and Compliance30%
3Cloud Technology and Services34%
4Billing, Pricing, and Support12%

Domain 3 (Cloud Technology and Services) and Domain 2 (Security and Compliance) together account for 64% of your score. If you're running short on study time in the days before the exam, prioritise these two domains. Domain 4 is the smallest slice — know the support plan tiers, Trusted Advisor, and EC2 pricing models well enough to handle the questions quickly.

Time Management on Exam Day

90 minutes for 65 questions gives you roughly 83 seconds per question. That sounds tight, but most questions are one or two sentences and a set of short answers — you won't be reading multi-paragraph case studies.

A Practical Pacing Strategy

  • Target the first pass in 60 minutes. That leaves a full 30 minutes to revisit flagged questions.
  • Flag and move. If a question stumps you, flag it and continue. Stalling for three minutes on one question is a poor trade.
  • Check the clock at question 33. You should have at least 45 minutes remaining at the halfway point. If you don't, pick up the pace on straightforward questions.
  • Never leave a question blank. The score is based on correct answers only — there's no penalty for a wrong answer. If time is almost up, guess.

What to Do With Flagged Questions

When you return to flagged questions, re-read them fresh. Often the answer that felt unclear becomes obvious after you've answered 20 more questions. If you're still torn between two options, ask yourself: which answer is more aligned with AWS best practices (least privilege, high availability, managed services over self-managed)? That framing resolves a surprising number of borderline questions.

Last-Week Preparation Checklist

The week before your exam is not the time to learn new concepts. It's the time to consolidate and build confidence.

Six to Seven Days Out

  • Run a full timed practice session using questions from all four domains. Note which domains cause the most hesitation — that's where to focus review. Our practice questions are organised by domain if you want targeted drilling.
  • Review the six pillars of the AWS Well-Architected Framework: Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization, and Sustainability.
  • Revisit the shared responsibility model. A common exam trick is to describe a scenario and ask whether the responsibility falls on AWS or the customer. AWS owns security of the cloud (hardware, global network, hypervisor, Availability Zones). You own security in the cloud (OS patches, IAM policies, data encryption, security group configuration).

Three to Four Days Out

  • Solidify your knowledge of core security services: CloudTrail (API call logging), GuardDuty (threat detection via ML), Inspector (vulnerability scanning for EC2, containers, and Lambda), and Shield (DDoS protection — Standard is free and automatic; Advanced is paid).
  • Make sure you can distinguish database types: RDS for relational workloads, DynamoDB for serverless NoSQL, Redshift for data warehousing, Aurora for high-throughput relational workloads.
  • Review EC2 pricing models, especially Spot Instances (up to ~90% discount but can be reclaimed by AWS with a 2-minute notice) and the difference between Standard and Convertible Reserved Instances.

The Day Before

  • Stop learning new material. Encountering an unfamiliar concept the night before is more likely to rattle your confidence than improve your score.
  • Confirm your testing logistics: appointment time, testing centre address (or online proctoring setup and system requirements), and what ID you need to bring.
  • Get a full night's sleep. Fatigue measurably degrades recall and decision-making — two skills you'll need for 90 minutes straight.

Morning of the Exam

  • Eat a normal breakfast. Low blood sugar makes sustained concentration harder.
  • Arrive at a testing centre 15 minutes early, or start your online proctoring check-in process at least 30 minutes before your scheduled start time. Technical issues happen.
  • You will not be allowed to bring notes, phones, or personal items into the testing space. For online proctored exams, you'll need a clean, quiet room and a webcam. Review the specific requirements on the exam registration platform well before exam day.
  • You'll get scratch paper or a whiteboard (physical centre) or an on-screen notepad (online). Use the first 60 seconds to jot down any frameworks you want handy — the six Well-Architected pillars, the six CAF perspectives, or the 6 Rs of migration, for example.

Reading the Questions Effectively

CLF-C02 questions are written to test conceptual understanding, not memorisation of specific numbers or ARN formats. Look for these patterns:

  • "Which service..." — they're testing whether you know what a service does. Eliminate services that clearly do something else.
  • "A company needs to..." — they're describing a business problem. Think about what AWS managed service solves it with the least operational overhead.
  • "Who is responsible for..." — almost certainly a shared responsibility question. Anchor your answer to the customer/AWS boundary.
  • Watch for absolutes. Words like "always," "never," and "automatically" in an answer choice often signal a distractor.

After the Exam

You'll typically see a pass/fail result on screen immediately after completing the exam. Your official score report, including domain-level breakdowns, is usually available within a few business days through the exam registration platform. The passing score is 700 on a scaled 100–1000 range — this is not a raw percentage, so a 700 doesn't mean you answered exactly 70% of questions correctly.

If you don't pass on the first attempt, the domain breakdown in your score report is invaluable. It tells you exactly where to focus for a retake. Many people pass on their second attempt with targeted review of one or two weak domains.

FAQ

How many questions do I need to get right to pass?

The CLF-C02 uses a scaled scoring system ranging from 100 to 1000, with a passing score of 700. Because of scaling, you cannot directly translate this to a specific number of correct answers. Focus on understanding concepts across all four domains rather than aiming for a raw percentage.

Can I flag questions and come back to them?

Yes. The testing interface allows you to flag questions for review and return to them before you submit. Use this feature aggressively — it's better to move forward and revisit than to stall on a hard question.

What happens if I run out of time?

If time expires, any questions you have not answered are treated as incorrect. Always make sure to answer every question before time runs out, even if you have to guess. There is no penalty for a wrong answer.

Is the CLF-C02 available online or only at a testing centre?

The exam is available both at physical Pearson VUE testing centres and via online proctoring. Check the current availability and system requirements on the exam registration platform when you schedule.

How long does it take to get my results?

A preliminary pass/fail result typically appears on screen immediately after you finish. Your full official score report, including domain performance, is generally available within a few business days through the registration platform.

What ID do I need to bring?

You'll need a valid, government-issued photo ID. For online proctored exams, you may need to photograph your ID as part of the check-in process. Verify the exact ID requirements on the exam registration platform when you book your appointment.
